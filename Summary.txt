PROJECT SUMMARY - IN VERY SIMPLE WORDS
====================================================================
(Written so that anyone can understand it - no tech knowledge needed.
 Everything here is based on the real files in this project.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT IS THIS PROJECT? (IN ONE LINE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
It is a computer helper for people who give money to new companies.

When someone is thinking about investing money in a small young
company (a "startup"), they first need to ask that company a lot of
careful questions to make sure it is a good, honest, safe bet. This
is called "due diligence" - basically "doing your homework before
you hand over money."

This project does that homework for you. It:

  1. Writes the 10 most important questions you should ask the company.
  2. Asks those questions one by one, listens to the answers, and
     asks a smart extra question whenever an answer sounds unclear
     or worrying.
  3. At the end, it writes a neat report that gives the company a
     score, points out the good things, the risky things, the
     missing information, and any answers that don't match each
     other - and suggests what to do next.

Think of it as a very well-prepared assistant who has read the
"how to check a startup" handbook, sits in on the meeting, takes
perfect notes, and hands you a summary afterwards.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT PROBLEM DOES IT SOLVE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Checking a startup properly is slow, tiring, and easy to mess up.
A person doing it has to:

  - Remember every important thing to ask.
  - Catch it when an answer is vague, or sounds risky, or is
    different from something the founder said five minutes ago.
  - Keep track of the whole long conversation.
  - Turn all of that into a fair, clear opinion.

People forget things. People get tired. People miss warning signs.

This project does all of that the same careful way every single
time. It never forgets a question, never loses track of the
conversation, and always writes the report in the same clear format.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHO IS IT FOR?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  - People and firms who invest money in startups (investors,
    venture capital analysts, angel investors).
  - Groups that look at many startups and want to check them all
    in the same fair way.
  - Founders themselves, who can use it to practise - to see the
    hard questions an investor will throw at them before the real
    meeting.

Important honest note: right now this is only the "engine" of the
product. There is no nice website, no login screen, and it is not
put online yet. The project's own notes say the website, login, and
online setup are "not built yet." What works today is the thinking
part behind the scenes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW DOES IT WORK? (STEP BY STEP, IN PLAIN WORDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

GETTING READY (done one time):
  The tool goes to a public webpage that contains a professional
  "how to check a startup" checklist. It copies that guide, cuts it
  into small readable pieces, and keeps those pieces in a little
  library it can search through quickly. This library is its
  "expert knowledge."

PART 1 - Make the 10 questions:
  a. You type in a few lines about the company. Example:
     "We are a small software company, 15 staff, 2 crore rupees
      in yearly sales."
  b. The tool searches its library for the parts of the checklist
     that matter most for this kind of company.
  c. It gives the company details plus those checklist pieces to
     the AI and asks: "What are the 10 best questions to ask this
     company next?"
  d. It cleans up the list, removes repeats, and puts the questions
     in order from most important to least. You now have exactly
     10 questions.

PART 2 - Have the conversation:
  e. A new "session" (one company's interview) is started and the
     10 questions are saved.
  f. The founder answers one question at a time. Every answer is
     saved.
  g. After each answer, the AI thinks: "Was that answer good enough,
     or should I dig deeper?"
       - If it needs more: it asks ONE targeted extra question about
         exactly what the founder just said. (Example: if the answer
         shows the company depends on just a few big customers, it
         asks what they are doing about that risk.)
       - If the answer was fine: it moves on to the next question.
  h. It never asks the same thing twice. If an answer clashes with
     something said earlier, it points that out and asks the founder
     to explain.
  i. So the interview always ends, there is a limit of 3 extra
     questions per original question.
  j. Once all 10 questions (and any extras) are answered, the
     interview is marked finished.

PART 3 - Write the report:
  k. The tool gathers the whole conversation.
  l. It checks its library again for anything useful.
  m. The AI reads the entire interview and writes up: a short
     summary, the strong points, the risky points, the missing
     information, any answers that contradict each other, an
     opinion on 10 different parts of the business, and suggested
     next steps.
  n. Then a set of FIXED RULES (not the AI) takes over and:
       - ignores fake "contradictions" and turns the serious ones
         into clearly listed risks,
       - turns each opinion into a number from 0 to 10,
       - combines those numbers into one overall score,
       - decides an overall risk level: LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, or
         CRITICAL.
  o. The finished report is checked, saved, and ready to read.

The reason the AI does not decide the final score itself: so the
numbers are consistent and can be checked. The AI only gives
opinions in words; the maths is done by rules that never change
their mind.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. MAIN FEATURES (THE SHORT LIST)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Automatically writes the top 10 questions for any startup, each
    with a topic, an importance level, and a reason why it matters.
  - Based on a real published checklist, not just the AI guessing.
  - A live one-question-at-a-time interview with smart follow-up
    questions that react to the actual answers.
  - Spots contradictions - when answers don't match each other or
    don't match the company details.
  - Never repeats a question.
  - A safety limit so the interview always ends.
  - A full written report with: summary, strengths (with proof),
    risks (with how bad and why), missing information, contradictions,
    scores for 10 business areas, one overall score, one risk level,
    and recommended next steps.
  - "Proof required" rule: if the founder claims something but shows
    no evidence, it is treated as missing information, NOT as a
    strong point.
  - The scores are worked out by fixed rules, so they are fair and
    repeatable.
  - You can safely ask for the report twice - it just gives you the
    one that already exists instead of making a second one.
  - Everything is saved on the computer: questions, answers, extra
    questions, and the final report.
  - Comes with automatic self-checks (tests) for all three parts.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT ARE PHASE 1, PHASE 2, AND PHASE 3?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The project was built in three steps, each one adding more:

  PHASE 1 - "The 10 Questions."
    The simplest version. You give company info, you get back 10
    ranked questions. No interview, nothing saved. Can be run with
    a single command.

  PHASE 2 - "The Interview."
    Takes those 10 questions and turns them into a back-and-forth
    conversation. The founder answers one at a time; the AI decides
    whether to ask a follow-up or move on. Adds a small database to
    remember the whole conversation. Phase 1 is reused as-is.

  PHASE 3 - "The Report."
    After the interview is done, it reads the whole thing and
    produces the scored, evidence-based report. Adds the scoring
    rules, the risk logic, and report storage. Reuses the library
    from Phase 1 and the saved conversation from Phase 2.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE FOUR TECH WORDS, EXPLAINED IN ONE LINE EACH
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  RAG:
    A method where the tool first looks up trusted reference
    material and then hands it to the AI, so the AI's questions and
    opinions are based on a real checklist instead of guesswork.

  ChromaDB:
    The small searchable library on the computer that stores the
    checklist in little pieces and quickly finds the pieces that
    fit a given company.

  NVIDIA LLM:
    The AI brain (a large language model) that actually writes the
    questions, decides the follow-ups during the interview, and
    reads the finished interview to produce the findings.

  SQLite:
    The simple file on the computer that remembers each interview -
    its questions, the founder's answers, the follow-ups, and the
    saved report.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT DOES THE INVESTOR GET AT THE END?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
One clear report about the startup, containing:

  - An overall score from 0 to 10.
  - An overall risk level: LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, or CRITICAL.
    (If even one CRITICAL red flag is found, the whole report is
     marked CRITICAL - even if everything else looks fine.)
  - A short plain-language summary of whether the company looks
    ready for investment.
  - A list of strengths, each backed by something the founder
    actually said or showed.
  - A list of risks, each with how serious it is and why it matters.
  - A list of missing information - what the founder did not
    properly answer, and why that gap is a problem.
  - A list of answers that contradicted each other.
  - A 0 to 10 score for 10 parts of the business: Market, Product,
    Traction (are people actually using/buying it), Business Model,
    Financials, Team, Competition, Technology, Go-To-Market (how
    they sell), and Risk.
  - A list of clear recommended next steps.

In short: a fast, fair, evidence-based second opinion to help
someone decide whether to invest.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
9. A REAL-LIFE EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
An investor is thinking about putting money into a company called
"Acme", which sells software to other businesses.

  1. The investor types in:
     "Acme is a small business-software company, 15 staff,
      2 crore rupees yearly sales, very early stage."

  2. The tool produces 10 sharp questions - for example: show 12
     months of bank statements to prove the sales figure; who owns
     the company and how; who owns the software/technology; how
     dependent are they on their biggest customers; how fast are
     they spending money and how long will it last; what is the
     hiring plan.

  3. The founder goes through the interview. When the founder says
     "70% of our income comes from our top five customers," the
     tool immediately asks a follow-up: "What are you doing to
     reduce that dependence?" Later, when the founder says sales
     are "3 crore," the tool notices this doesn't match the earlier
     "2 crore" and asks them to explain.

  4. After all questions are answered, the tool writes the report.
     It might give an overall score of 5.8 and a risk level of HIGH.
     It lists "good sales growth for such an early company" as a
     strength; lists "too dependent on a few customers" and "sales
     numbers don't match" as HIGH risks; notes "no information given
     about how many customers stay or leave" as a gap; and
     recommends "get properly audited accounts and a signed
     ownership document before going ahead."

  5. The investor reads this in a few minutes and uses it to decide:
     go ahead, ask for better terms, or walk away.
